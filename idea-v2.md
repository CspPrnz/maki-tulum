# Maki Tulum — Sharpened Product Thesis

> Build a premium direct-booking brand for a small Tulum compound that uses OTAs for first-touch discovery and converts great stays into repeat direct relationships.

---

## 1. The actual business

Maki Tulum is not primarily a software company. It is a hospitality business with a distribution strategy.

The strategy is:

- Use **Airbnb / Booking.com / VRBO / other channels** to acquire first-time guests.
- Deliver a stay that feels more personal, more trustworthy, and better planned than the OTA experience.
- Convert those guests into **repeat direct bookers on `makitulum.com`**.
- Increase margin, ownership of guest relationship, and repeat rate over time.

This is a much stronger thesis than "go direct-only" from day one. OTAs are useful for demand capture, especially early. The mistake is not using them. The mistake is letting them own the guest forever.

So the core business question is not:

> "Can we replace OTAs immediately?"

It is:

> "Can we use OTAs to fill the funnel, then make direct the obvious choice for stay number two?"

That is the right frame.

---

## 2. Core thesis

Maki wins if it becomes a place guests **discover on OTAs, remember as a hosted experience, and rebook directly because direct feels better**.

Direct should win on:

- better service
- better planning help
- better relationship
- better repeat-guest treatment
- better booking confidence
- slightly better economics for the guest, without cheapening the brand

Direct should not win on:

- aggressive discounting
- fake scarcity
- gimmicky AI
- trying to out-OTA the OTAs

The product is therefore not "a website with booking." The product is a **direct relationship layer** around the property.

---

## 3. Positioning

### One-line

Maki Tulum is a small, design-led jungle compound in Aldea Zama where guests can first discover the stay anywhere, but the best way to return is direct.

### Stronger positioning statement

Maki Tulum offers calm, design-forward stays for guests who want Tulum without the chaos: a better-hosted week, in multiple languages, with named people, honest guidance, and a direct line to the property before and during the trip.

### What makes it different

- A small compound can feel more personal than a hotel and more trustworthy than a random short-term rental.
- The owner has a credible **German-language wedge** in a market where that is uncommon.
- The property can build trust through specificity: real people, real routines, real local partners, real caveats.
- Repeat guests are highly valuable in hospitality, and this concept is structurally aimed at them.

---

## 4. The real moat

The moat is not custom software. The moat is the combination of:

- a memorable physical property
- excellent visual assets
- a distinct owner voice
- multilingual hosting
- fast WhatsApp communication
- repeat-guest memory and recognition
- a better pre-arrival and in-stay experience
- specific, trustworthy local recommendations

That combination is hard to copy because it depends on real operations and real taste, not just features.

---

## 5. Business model and channel strategy

### Channel model

Maki should be **multi-channel by design**:

- OTAs for first booking and occupancy support
- direct for repeat bookings and margin expansion
- content/social/email/WhatsApp for retention and referral

### Distribution principle

OTAs are acquisition channels, not loyalty channels.

The repeat-guest loop should look like this:

1. Guest discovers Maki on Airbnb, Booking.com, VRBO, Instagram, or search.
2. Guest books wherever trust is highest for trip one.
3. Guest has an unusually smooth stay: communication, arrival, recommendations, service, follow-through.
4. Guest leaves with a direct relationship to Maki, not just to a platform listing.
5. Guest receives post-stay follow-up, return invitation, and reasons to book direct next time.
6. Trip two happens on `makitulum.com`.

### What direct needs to offer repeat guests

- preferred repeat-guest pricing or perks
- easier rebooking
- remembered preferences
- direct WhatsApp planning help
- first access to dates or unit combinations
- bundled experiences

Important: direct does not need to be dramatically cheaper. It needs to feel **better, easier, and more personal**.

---

## 6. The product, properly scoped

There are two separate products here:

1. The **guest-facing direct brand and booking experience**
2. The **internal operating stack**

For v1, Maki should build the first and mostly buy the second.

### Build in v1

- premium marketing site
- editorial unit pages
- direct booking flow
- clear availability and all-in pricing
- WhatsApp-first communication
- repeat-guest capture and rebooking flows
- guide/content layer
- review aggregation and trust pages

### Buy in v1

- PMS / channel manager
- payment infrastructure
- guest verification
- smart lock integrations
- housekeeping workflows
- maintenance ticketing
- pre-arrival guest app if needed

### Avoid in v1

- custom role-based back office
- custom housekeeping system
- custom channel sync engine
- AI concierge with transactional logic
- custom loyalty system

If a tool like Hostaway, Hospitable, Lodgify, Guesty, Enso Connect, Chekin, or Truvi covers 80% of the need, buy it. Only build where Maki can be genuinely differentiated.

---

## 7. The guest promise

The direct experience should promise four things:

### 1. Confidence

Guests know exactly what they are booking, what it costs, who is hosting them, and what the property is and is not.

### 2. Taste

The site, photography, recommendations, and copy should communicate discernment. The property should feel curated, not generic.

### 3. Care

Fast replies, human warmth, remembered preferences, and smooth pre-arrival planning should make guests feel looked after.

### 4. Continuity

The relationship should persist after checkout, so the second booking is easier than the first.

---

## 8. Why this is a good idea

This is a good idea if the property is genuinely good and the team can execute hospitality well.

Reasons it is attractive:

- Direct repeat bookings materially improve margins.
- Small compounds benefit disproportionately from trust and narrative.
- Tulum is a high-emotion destination where planning help matters.
- A multilingual, owner-led brand can feel more human than a platform listing.
- Returning guests are more profitable, lower-friction, and often higher-trust.

In other words: this idea compounds. The first stay acquires the guest. The second stay is where the economics get much better.

---

## 9. Why this could fail

This fails if Maki confuses a branding opportunity with a software project.

Primary failure modes:

- The property is not differentiated enough in real life.
- The team overbuilds tech instead of improving operations and media.
- OTA guests leave satisfied but not attached to the brand.
- The direct site looks beautiful but does not reduce booking friction.
- There is no repeat-guest CRM discipline after checkout.
- The market message is too broad and sounds like every other "luxury sanctuary in Tulum."

The biggest hidden risk is this:

If the guest experience is merely good, OTAs will keep winning. Direct only wins when the stay creates memory, trust, and a desire to come back to the same people.

---

## 10. What success actually requires

To make this work, Maki needs strength in five areas.

### 1. Property quality

The compound has to be visually distinctive, comfortable, and genuinely desirable.

### 2. Operational discipline

Turnovers, maintenance, arrivals, guest replies, and issue resolution must be excellent. Direct loyalty is earned operationally.

### 3. Media quality

Photography, video, floor plans, and possibly 3D walkthroughs need to remove uncertainty and sell atmosphere.

### 4. Retention system

The business needs a repeat-guest engine:

- guest data captured cleanly
- consented email/WhatsApp follow-up
- memorable post-stay touchpoints
- repeat-booking offer or privilege

### 5. Focused scope

The team must resist building software that does not clearly move occupancy, ADR, conversion, repeat rate, or ops reliability.

## 11. Sharpened v1

The original concept is directionally right but too broad. A better v1 is:

### Guest-facing v1

- Home page with strong editorial narrative
- Unit pages with excellent media and honest details
- Persistent booking CTA with all-in pricing transparency
- OTA-safe channel strategy in the background
- Review aggregation with freshness dates
- WhatsApp contact as a primary support surface
- Guide section with a small number of genuinely strong articles
- Repeat-guest capture at checkout and post-stay

### Ops v1

- Buy a PMS / channel manager
- Buy verification
- Buy guest messaging support if needed
- Buy housekeeping workflow if needed
- Use a lightweight owner dashboard or even reporting pulled from existing tools

### Nice-to-have but not day one

- Matterport
- interactive compound map
- bundle builder
- no-card date hold
- pre-arrival PWA

### Not v1

- AI concierge
- custom multi-role admin platform
- bespoke loyalty system

---

## 12. Sharpened v2

Once v1 is live and producing bookings, add things that deepen the repeat loop:

- repeat-guest login or magic-link rebooking
- remembered preferences
- direct-only bundled experiences
- retreat / multi-unit quoting flow
- stronger lifecycle messaging
- compound map
- richer pre-arrival planning flow

Only after direct demand is clearly working should Maki consider building more proprietary tooling.

## 13. What I would add to the idea

### A retention thesis

The original document talks well about booking, but the real edge is rebooking. That deserves its own section.

Add:

- how guest data will be captured
- what the post-stay sequence is
- what the repeat guest receives
- how the team measures second-stay conversion

### A business truth section

Before building, document:

- number of units
- current occupancy
- ADR by season
- current OTA mix
- repeat rate today
- average stay length
- contribution margin by channel

Without this, it is hard to judge what is worth building.

### A clear ICP

The audience list is decent, but it still mixes several audiences. Pick one primary ICP for launch.

Recommendation:

Primary ICP: 30-50 year-old design-conscious couples and small groups from the US, Germany, Austria, and Switzerland who want a calmer, more thoughtful Tulum stay and are likely to return if hosted well.

Retreats and larger groups can be a second wedge, but should not dominate the initial message.

## 14. What I would remove or downgrade

- Most of the internal dashboard ambition from the core concept
- Anything that smells like building a startup product around the property
- Heavy emphasis on vendor-stat uplift percentages
- AI concierge as a signature bet
- Direct-only positioning as a launch dogma

Those elements either add complexity too early or distract from the real edge.

---

## 15. What makes this a great idea

This becomes a great idea when Maki is framed not as a nicer booking site, but as a **repeat-guest machine**.

That means:

- first stay can happen anywhere
- second stay should happen direct
- every guest touchpoint should support that transition

The website is part of that.
The stay is part of that.
The follow-up is part of that.
The local network is part of that.

Great hospitality businesses do not merely convert traffic. They compound trust.

That should be the organizing idea.

---

## 16. Recommended strategic principles

1. **Use OTAs deliberately.** Treat them as acquisition, not defeat.
2. **Build brand where it matters.** The public site should feel differentiated and memorable.
3. **Buy commodity software.** Build only the direct experience and retention edge.
4. **Obsess over trip two.** The second booking is the real business model.
5. **Prefer specificity over aspiration.** Name people, routines, caveats, and practices.
6. **Measure channel economics.** Do not let aesthetics obscure the numbers.

---

## 17. Suggested north-star metric

The most important strategic metric is not just direct share.

It is:

**Percent of eligible repeat guests who rebook direct within 24 months.**

That metric captures whether Maki is actually building a brand relationship instead of renting demand from platforms.

Supporting metrics:

- first-time booking mix by channel
- direct share of total bookings
- repeat rate
- repeat-direct rate
- WhatsApp response time
- review score and review volume
- bundle attach rate
- net revenue by channel

---

## 18. Recommended next decisions

Before design or build, answer these clearly:

1. How many units are in scope, exactly?
2. Which PMS/channel manager will serve as source of truth?
3. What is the repeat-guest offer for direct booking?
4. What guest data and consent flows are legally and operationally available?
5. Who on the ground owns guest communication every day?
6. What media assets already exist, and what needs to be shot?
7. Which language combination launches first?
